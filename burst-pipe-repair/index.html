Shut off the main water valve, which is usually near the water meter, in the garage, a utility closet or along the front wall of the house. Turn it clockwise until it stops.

Why pipes burst

Freezes, pressure and corrosion

Freezing is the best-known cause. Water expands as it turns to ice, and the pipe splits where the ice forms or where the pressure builds behind it. The 2021 winter storm showed how many Texas homes have exposed lines in attics, garages and outside walls.

High water pressure and old pipe also cause bursts. A pressure regulator that has failed can send pressure above what the fixtures are built to handle, and corroded galvanized or aging copper can give way without any freeze at all.

Repair

How a burst pipe is repaired

The plumber finds the split, cuts out the damaged section and replaces it with new pipe and fittings. Copper may be joined with a soldered or press fitting, and PEX with crimp or expansion fittings.

PipeTypical repairNotes
CopperCut out the section, install a couplingCheck nearby sections for pitting or damage
PEXReplace the section with new fittingsOften more freeze tolerant than rigid pipe
CPVCReplace the sectionBecomes brittle with age
Galvanized steelReplace the section, consider repipingAnother failure may follow soon

If several sections have split or the pipe is old, a repipe may cost less over time. For a small pinhole leak that has not burst yet, leak detection finds it before it floods.

After the water

Drying, insurance and prevention

Water damage grows quickly, so begin drying right away with fans and open windows if the weather allows. Photograph everything, keep the plumber's written report and call your insurer, since coverage for sudden burst damage is usually treated differently from slow seepage.

To reduce the chance of a freeze burst, insulate exposed lines, open cabinet doors under sinks on cold nights and know where your main shutoff is before you need it. Letting a faucet drip during a hard freeze helps keep water moving.
