Sewer backup: what to do first

A sewer backup is a health hazard, because wastewater carries bacteria and should not be handled bare-handed. Stop using sinks, showers, the washer and toilets, since each gallon you add has no place to go.

A tub or a floor drain that fills when the washer runs, or a toilet that bubbles when you flush another fixture, means the main line is blocked or broken downstream. Call us and tell the dispatcher what is backing up so the right equipment arrives.

Protect the house while you wait

Move rugs and electronics out of the wet area, open windows if the smell is strong and keep children and pets away. Do not run a plunger hard on a backed-up tub, because it can push wastewater back through other drains.

Causes

What breaks a sewer line

Roots are the most common cause. They enter through joints and cracks, grow into a mass and trap paper and grease until the line stops.

Age and material matter too. Older homes can have clay tile with many joints, fiber pipe that has flattened or cast iron that has rusted from the inside, while newer plastic pipe can be damaged by heavy equipment or a shifting slab. North Texas clay expands and shrinks with moisture, and that movement can pull a joint apart or put a belly in the line.

Repair options

Trenchless lining, pipe bursting or a dig

A camera inspection decides the repair. A trench-free approach such as a cured-in-place liner can seal cracks and joints without opening the yard, while pipe bursting pulls a new pipe through the old path. A section that has collapsed or sagged usually needs a dig to replace it.

Each method has limits, and a plumber should show you the video and explain why one fits better than another. A liner does not fix a belly or a collapsed pipe, and it needs a clear path to reach the damage.

Your side

Who owns which part of the line

The property owner is usually responsible for the private lateral that runs from the house to where it meets the city main, and the city handles the main in the street. Where that handoff sits varies by address, so ask the City of Euless public works department before you dig or sign for a large repair.

If a blockage is in the city main, the city can clear it, and a camera on the private side shows the plumber and you whether the problem is yours. If the property is on a septic system instead of the city sewer, tell the dispatcher so the right plumber is matched.

Prevention

After the repair: keeping the line healthy

A repaired line stays healthy longer when you keep grease out of kitchen drains, flush only toilet paper and plan a periodic sewer line cleaning if roots are a repeat problem. Trees planted close to the lateral are a common long-term cause.

If you are buying a home, a sewer inspection before closing shows what condition the line is in. For ongoing grease buildup, hydro jetting restores the pipe wall.
