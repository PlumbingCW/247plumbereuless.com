Signs

Signs of a failing water service line

A wet or sunken spot in the yard when it has not rained, a meter that moves with every tap off, a sudden drop in pressure or a bill that has jumped are the usual signs. Callers also report muddy water or a sound of rushing water near the meter.

Urgent

Water line leak emergency: what to shut off

A broken service line can release a large amount of water quickly, and it floods the yard or a crawl space. Shut off the main valve at the house, and use the home shutoff first rather than the utility's meter valve.

If water is in the yard and you cannot find the home shutoff, tell the dispatcher and ask the utility about the meter valve. Our dispatcher can send a plumber the same day when one is available.

Before the plumber arrives

Close the house main, keep children and pets away from the soft ground and mark the wet area with a flag or a hose so the plumber can find it. Do not dig near the leak yourself, because buried gas and electric lines may be close.

Ownership

Who owns which part of the line

Utilities draw the line differently, so check yours. The City of Hurst, for example, states that it owns the line from the main to the meter and the customer owns the line from the meter to the home. Ask the City of Euless public works department how that boundary applies to your address.

A leak on the city side is reported to the utility, and a leak on the private side is the owner's repair. A pressure test and a locator help the plumber show which side the leak is on.

Repair

Spot repair, trench or pipe bursting

A leak at a single spot can be repaired by digging to it and replacing the damaged section. A line that has failed in several places, or that is made of old galvanized steel or thin plastic, is usually replaced.

Pressure

Low pressure that is not a leak

Low pressure is not always a leak. A failing pressure regulator, a partly closed valve, a clogged filter or scale in old pipe can reduce flow, and so can a utility maintenance event. The plumber tests static pressure at a hose bib to find where the pressure drops.

If the pressure is normal at the meter and low in the house, the loss is in the line or the pipes, and our leak detection or repiping pages explain the next steps.
