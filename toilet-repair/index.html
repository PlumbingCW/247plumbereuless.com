Toilet overflowing: stop the water

Turn the supply valve behind the toilet clockwise until it stops. If the valve is stuck, lift the float arm in the tank or put a hand on the flapper to hold it closed, then dry the floor.

An overflowing toilet that comes up again after a plunger points to a deeper stoppage, and a toilet that overflows together with a tub or floor drain points to the main line. Call and tell the dispatcher which you see so the right plumber is sent.

While you wait

Close the supply valve, put towels around the base, switch off the bathroom light if water has reached a fixture, and keep the door closed so pets stay out. Avoid chemical drain openers in a toilet.

Common repairs

Running, leaking and weak-flushing toilets

A toilet that runs on usually has a worn flapper, a misadjusted fill valve or a chain that is too tight or too loose. A weak flush may have clogged rim jets or a partial blockage.

Runs every few minutes

A leaking flapper lets tank water slip into the bowl.

Water at the base

A failed wax ring or a loose bolt lets water escape under the toilet.

Rocks when you sit

A loose toilet or a damaged flange breaks the seal.

Tank sweats or drips

Condensation or a loose tank bolt can wet the floor.

Most of these are a short repair when the toilet is otherwise sound. A cracked bowl or tank cannot be repaired and needs replacement.

Flange

The toilet flange and wax ring

The flange is the ring on the floor that holds the toilet and connects it to the drain. When it cracks, rusts or sits below the finished floor, the wax ring cannot seal, and water escapes at the base with every flush.

A plumber can repair a flange with a repair ring or replace it, and then set a new wax ring and tighten the bolts evenly. Leaking at the base often means the floor underneath has been getting wet, so check for soft spots before the new toilet is set.

Installation

Installing a new toilet

1

Shut off and remove

Water is shut off, the old toilet is lifted and the flange cleaned.

2

Check the flange

The flange is inspected and repaired if cracked or low.

3

Set the new toilet

A new wax ring or gasket and bolts seal the base.

4

Connect and test

The supply is connected and leak-tested before you use it.

A comfort-height bowl, a water-saving model or a dual-flush unit are the usual choices, and the rough-in distance from the wall to the drain decides which toilets fit. Measure it before buying, or ask the plumber to check.
