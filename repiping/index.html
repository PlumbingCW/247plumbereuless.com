When to repipe

Signs your supply lines are failing

One leak does not mean the house needs new pipes. A pattern does: leaks in several places, rust-colored water, falling pressure and a home built long enough ago that its pipe material is nearing the end of its life.

Repeated leaks

A second or third pinhole in a short time says the pipe is failing along its length.

Brown or rusty water

Corrosion inside galvanized steel sheds rust into the water.

Low pressure at every tap

Scale narrows old pipe until the flow drops.

Pipe material known to fail

Galvanized steel and polybutylene are common candidates for replacement.

Materials

Galvanized, copper, polybutylene and PEX

Galvanized steel corrodes from the inside and often narrows the bore over the decades. Polybutylene, used widely from the late 1970s into the 1990s, has a record of failing at fittings. Copper lasts a long time but can pit in aggressive water.

PEX is the common replacement today because it flexes around corners, needs fewer joints and resists freezing damage better than rigid pipe. Copper remains a good choice where code or preference calls for it.

The job

What a repipe involves

1

Survey the house

Routes, access points and fixture count are mapped.

2

Plan the routes

New lines go through walls, attic or crawl space where possible.

3

Install new lines

Water is shut off in stages and new pipe is connected.

4

Test and patch

Pressure tests prove the system, then drywall is patched.

A repipe usually takes a few days for an average house. Water is off for part of each day, and the plumber will tell you when. Drywall cuts are patched, though painting is a separate job.

In Euless slab homes, new lines often go through the attic and walls instead of under the foundation, which avoids breaking concrete. A leaking line under the slab is a different job, covered on our slab leak page.

Water quality

Why the water runs brown during or after a repipe

Brown or cloudy water after work on the lines is common. Disturbed sediment and loosened scale flow out when the system is refilled, and it usually clears after the taps run for several minutes.

If the water stays discolored after that, tell the plumber. A persistent color can point to a remaining section of old galvanized pipe, a water heater full of sediment or a problem outside the house, and each has a different fix.

Alternatives

Spot repair or whole-house repipe

A single failed section on an otherwise sound system should be repaired, not replaced. If the same line has failed twice, or the house has galvanized or polybutylene pipe throughout, repairs become a recurring cost.

Our leak detection visit shows how many lines are leaking and helps you decide between a spot repair and a repipe. Ask the City of Euless building department about permit requirements for your address before work begins.
