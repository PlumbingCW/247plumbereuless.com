Slab Leak Warning Signs in Euless, TX Homes

A slab leak is a leak in a water line that runs under or through the concrete foundation. It is hard to see and easy to miss, which is why knowing the signs matters.

This guide lists what a slab leak looks, sounds and feels like, and explains what to do first.

Overview

What a slab leak is

In many North Texas homes, water supply lines run in or beneath the slab to reach the kitchen, bath and water heater. When one of those lines corrodes, wears or is stressed by foundation movement, water leaks into the soil and the concrete.

Because the leak is under the floor, there is often no visible damage until it has run for a while. That is why the first signs are usually on the bill and underfoot, not on the ceiling.

Signs

Warning signs to watch for

A warm spot on the floor

A hot-water line leaking under the slab warms the concrete above it.

The meter moves with everything off

Water is leaving the system.

The sound of running water

You hear it when the house is quiet.

A higher water bill

A jump with no change in use.

Damp or buckling flooring

Water is rising through the slab.

Cracks in the foundation or walls

A leak can wash out soil or movement can be the cause.

Any one of these can have another explanation, but two or more together make a slab leak likely.

Why North Texas

Clay soil and the slab

North Texas clay swells when wet and shrinks in drought, and slabs move with it. That movement can stress lines under the foundation, and a pipe that rubs against concrete or gravel can wear through.

A new leak after a long dry spell followed by heavy rain is not unusual, which is why homeowners often notice slab leaks in the months after the weather swings.

First steps

What to do if you suspect a slab leak

1

Run the meter test

Turn off everything and see if the meter moves.

2

Check for a warm floor spot

Walk barefoot and feel for warm patches.

3

Shut off the main if water is rising

Stop the flow and call.

4

Call a plumber

Describe the signs, and have the bills ready.

Do not break the concrete yourself. A plumber can pinpoint the leak with listening gear and thermal imaging so the opening is small.

Rule out

Other causes of a damp or warm floor

Not every warm or damp spot is a slab leak. A warm patch can come from a heating duct under the slab, a water heater closet that shares a wall, or sun on a window. A damp spot can come from a plumbing leak in a wall, a condensation problem or a drainage issue outside.

The meter test is the best way to separate them. If the meter stays still with all water off, a slab leak is unlikely, and the cause is elsewhere. If it moves, the leak is real, and the next question is where.

A plumber can also run a pressure test on the hot and cold lines separately, which shows whether the leak is in a hot line, a cold line or a branch.

Questions

Questions to ask a plumber about a slab leak

Ask how the leak will be located and whether the plumber will mark it on the floor before any cutting. Ask which repair options apply to your house, with a price range for each, and what flooring or drywall will be disturbed.

Ask for a written report with the leak location, the findings and photos, and ask whether the repair carries a warranty and what it covers. A clear answer to each question tells you the plumber has a plan and not just a guess.

Repair paths

The right option depends on the leak location, the pipe age and the flooring above. See repiping if the system is old, and leak detection to confirm the leak before you decide.

Checklist

A one-page checklist to share with your plumber

  • The date you first noticed a sign, such as a warm spot or a bill jump.
  • Meter readings from the test, with the time of each reading.
  • Locations of any warm or damp floor areas, with photos.
  • The age of the house and the type of supply pipe, if known.
  • Your homeowner's policy details, including any leak endorsement.

Having this on one page lets the plumber confirm the leak faster and lets you start an insurance conversation with facts. It also gives you a record to compare if the leak returns or if a second opinion is needed.

Mistakes

Mistakes that make a slab leak worse

Waiting to see if a warm spot goes away is the most common. A leaking hot-water line will not heal, and the soil under the slab keeps washing out while you wait.

Drilling or cutting the floor to look for the leak yourself is the other. Without a locator you are likely to miss and damage the floor you were trying to save.

Local notes

Insurance, permits and licensed help

Call your insurer early. Coverage for slab leaks varies by policy, and the plumber's written report and photos support a claim.

Euless notes on permits, licensing and digging

Ask the City of Euless building department whether a reroute or repipe needs a permit at your address. Rules depend on the work, and a plumber should be able to explain what is usual.

Verify any plumber's license through the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners public search before work starts. Before any digging on the property, call 811, the national call-before-you-dig number, so buried utilities are marked.
