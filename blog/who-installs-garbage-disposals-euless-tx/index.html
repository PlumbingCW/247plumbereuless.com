Who Installs Garbage Disposals? A Euless, TX Homeowner Guide

A garbage disposal sits at the crossroads of plumbing and electrical work, which is why homeowners wonder who should install it. The short answer is that a licensed plumber can handle the whole job, including the drain and the electrical connection in many homes.

This guide explains who does what, what an installation involves and how to choose a unit that fits your sink.

Who

Plumber, electrician or do it yourself

A plumber installs the disposal, connects it to the sink flange and the drain and links the dishwasher hose. Many disposals plug into an outlet under the sink, and if one is already there the plumber can finish the job in a single visit.

If there is no outlet, an electrician may be needed to add one. A switch for the disposal is also an electrical item, and a disposal that is hardwired needs an electrician or a plumber who is licensed to do that work.

The job

What a disposal installation involves

1

Shut off power and water

Switch off the breaker and close the shutoff valves.

2

Remove the old unit

Disconnect the drain, the dishwasher hose and the mounting ring.

3

Mount the new unit

Seat the flange, set the mount and lock the disposal in place.

4

Connect and test

Reconnect the drain and hose, restore power and run water.

A typical replacement takes an hour or two. Adding a disposal where none existed takes longer, because the sink flange, the drain and the electrical supply all change.

Choosing

Horsepower, feed type and noise

A one third horsepower unit is fine for a single person, and a half to three quarter horsepower unit handles a family. Higher horsepower grinds more finely and runs more quietly.

A continuous-feed model starts when you flip the switch, while a batch-feed model works when the stopper is in place. Batch feed is safer around children, and continuous feed is more common.

Check the size under your sink before you buy. A big unit can crowd a small cabinet or hit the trap, and the plumber can tell you what fits.

Before you call

Checks that make the visit faster

Look under the sink and note whether there is an outlet and a switch, how much clearance there is below the sink and what kind of dishwasher connection you have. A photo of the cabinet interior helps the dispatcher plan the visit.

If you already bought a unit, keep the box and the instruction sheet handy.

Troubleshooting

Common disposal problems and what they mean

A unit that hums and does not turn is jammed, and one that is silent is often tripped or dead. A leak at the top points to the sink flange seal, a leak at the side to a hose or clamp and a leak from the body to a failed unit.

Strange noises usually mean a foreign object such as a utensil or a bottle cap. Switch the power off and remove it with tongs, never with a hand. A disposal that smells is often coated with grease and food film, and a cleaning with ice and citrus peel can help between visits.

If problems repeat every few weeks, the cause may be the unit's age or a drain that is partly blocked beyond it. A plumber can run a cable through the line and test the disposal in the same visit.

Cost and time

How long and how much to expect

Plan on an hour or two for a straight replacement and longer where a new outlet or a new drain connection is needed. The price follows the unit you choose and the work around it, and a plumber can explain both on the phone once you describe the sink and the existing hookups.

Ask whether the quote includes haul-away of the old unit, a new dishwasher hose and a new power cord, because those small items are easy to leave off a low number and easy to add to a final bill. A written scope of work protects both sides.

  • Confirm the unit size fits the cabinet before the visit.
  • Ask whether the quote covers parts that are likely to be needed.
  • Keep the warranty paperwork for the disposal itself.
Mistakes

Mistakes that shorten a disposal's life

Running the disposal without water, grinding large amounts of food at once and pouring grease down the sink are the habits that wear out disposals early. A unit that is treated gently can last for many years.

Another is ignoring a small leak at the flange. A drip that reaches the cabinet floor will rot particleboard over time, so fix a leak the week you find it.

Care

Keeping a disposal working

Run cold water while the disposal runs and for several seconds after, and feed food in small amounts. Avoid grease, coffee grounds in bulk, fibrous items like celery and starchy foods like rice in large quantities.

If the sink is slow even when the disposal runs, the clog may be past the unit, and drain cleaning is the fix. For a jammed or leaking unit, see our garbage disposal repair page.

Euless notes on grease and the city sewer

Grease poured down a disposal ends up in your private drain line and then in the city's wastewater system. The City of Euless public works water and wastewater division is responsible for testing and maintaining the city's water and wastewater lines, according to the city.

Ask the division about any grease guidance for homes and businesses, and keep cooking fats in a container for the trash instead of the sink. For a restaurant or shop, our commercial plumbing page explains grease trap care.
