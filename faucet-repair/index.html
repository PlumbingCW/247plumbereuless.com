Urgent

Supply line or shutoff valve failing: stop the water

A supply line under a sink can split, and an old shutoff valve can crack or fail to close. Turn the valve to the right at the wall, and if it does not stop the flow, close the main water valve for the whole house.

A failed valve that will not shut off is a repair we treat as urgent, because the water will keep flowing until the line is replaced. Tell the dispatcher what you see so the plumber brings the right valve and fittings.

Shut it down

Turn the valve at the wall clockwise, or close the main valve if that fails. Place a bucket under the leak, dry the cabinet and open the cabinet doors so air can dry it. Take photos if there is damage, for your insurance record.

Common repairs

Faucets that drip, spray or lose pressure

A dripping faucet usually has a worn cartridge, seat or washer, depending on the type. A faucet that sprays sideways or has weak flow often has mineral buildup in the aerator or a clogged cartridge.

Shower and tub

Shower valves, showerheads and tub faucets

A tub faucet that drips after the water is off usually has a failed cartridge or washer inside the wall. A shower that goes hot and cold may have a worn pressure-balancing cartridge, and a showerhead that leaks at the arm needs new tape or a new fitting.

Some valves can be reached through an access panel, while others require opening the wall. A plumber will tell you which before work starts, so you know whether tile or drywall will be touched.

Outdoors

Outside faucets and hose bibs

An outdoor faucet that drips inside the wall after a freeze often has a split pipe behind it. Shutting off the interior valve to the hose bib and draining the line before winter helps prevent that.

If you see wet drywall behind a hose bib or a damp spot on the slab, call us, because a failed bib can leak into a wall for months. Our leak detection methods locate the source if the leak is not obvious.
