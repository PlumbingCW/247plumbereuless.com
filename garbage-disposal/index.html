Urgent

Disposal leaking or flooding the cabinet

A leak under the sink can come from the sink flange, the dishwasher hose, the drain connection or the disposal body. Water pooling in the cabinet can soak the floor and the particleboard under it quickly, so turn off the disposal and place a bucket under the leak.

If the leak is from the body itself, the seals have failed and the unit needs replacement. If it is from a connection, a repair often fixes it.

Before the plumber arrives

Switch the disposal off at the wall, unplug it if it is plugged in under the sink, and empty the cabinet so you can see the source. Do not run the dishwasher while the line is open.

Jam

Humming but not spinning

A disposal that hums but does not spin is jammed, often by a bone, a utensil or a fruit pit. Many units have a hex socket on the bottom where a wrench turns the blades and frees the jam, and a reset button on the base protects the motor.

Turn the power off before you try this, and never use your hand. If the unit keeps jamming, the motor may be worn or the blades damaged, and replacement is cheaper than repeated visits.

Replacement

Choosing and installing a new disposal

A typical home disposal runs from one third to one horsepower. Higher horsepower grinds food more finely and is quieter, and a continuous-feed model starts when the switch is on, while a batch-feed model works only with the stopper in place.

1

Disconnect

Power, the old unit and the drain connections are removed.

2

Mount

The new flange and unit are secured under the sink.

3

Connect

The drain, dishwasher hose and power are reconnected.

4

Test

Water and the unit run to check for leaks and noise.

If you are wondering who handles the installation, a plumber is the right person, and the plumber can handle electrical and drain connections together. We install a disposal you supply or help you pick one.

Prevention

What not to put in a disposal

Grease, coffee grounds, fibrous vegetables such as celery and starchy foods such as rice and pasta can clog the drain beyond the disposal. Run cold water while the disposal runs and for a short time after to move waste through the pipe.

If the sink backs up even after the disposal runs, the clog is beyond the unit and drain cleaning is the right next step. A line that clogs often may need hydro jetting to remove grease from the wall.
