Do You Have to Flush a Tankless Water Heater? What Euless Homes Need to Know

Yes, a tankless water heater needs to be flushed. It has no tank, but it does have a heat exchanger, and minerals in the water build up on it as scale.

This guide explains why scale matters, how often to flush and what the flush involves.

The answer

Why a tankless heater needs flushing

Water carries dissolved minerals, and heating it causes some of them to settle out as scale. In a tankless unit the scale forms inside the narrow passages of the heat exchanger, where it restricts flow and insulates the metal from the water.

Scale lowers efficiency, raises the temperature the exchanger runs at and eventually triggers error codes or failure. A flush dissolves the scale with a mild acid solution and rinses it out before it does that damage.

How often

Setting a flush schedule

Manufacturers commonly recommend a yearly flush, and hard water shortens the interval. The right schedule depends on your water, so test your tap and read the city's report before you decide.

The process

How a tankless flush works

1

Shut off power and gas

The heater is isolated before service.

2

Close the service valves

Hot and cold isolation valves are closed.

3

Connect a pump and hoses

A small pump circulates descaler through the unit.

4

Circulate and rinse

Solution runs for about an hour, then the system is rinsed.

Units installed with service valves are quick to flush. Units without them need valves added first, which is an inexpensive upgrade that makes every later service easier.

Do it yourself

Can you flush it yourself

Some homeowners flush their own units with a pump, hoses and white vinegar or a descaling solution recommended by the maker. If you do, follow the manual, shut off gas and power first and clear the inlet screen when you finish.

A plumber can also check the vent, the screens and the error history in the same visit. If the unit is not flowing well after a flush, there may be another problem such as a failing sensor.

Gas or electric

Differences between gas and electric tankless flushes

Gas units have a burner, a vent and a combustion system, so a service visit also looks at the burner, the flame sensor and the vent connections. Electric units have heating elements and a control board instead, and scale can coat the elements directly.

Either type uses the same basic flush, with a pump circulating solution through the heat exchanger. The extra checks are where a plumber adds value, because a vent leak or a dirty flame sensor will not show up in a flush alone.

If the unit has a built-in inlet filter screen, clear it each time. A clogged screen mimics the symptoms of scale and is a five-minute fix.

Solutions

Vinegar or a commercial descaler

White vinegar is the common homeowner choice and works well for light scale. A commercial descaler is stronger and may be needed for heavy buildup, but it must be compatible with the heat exchanger material, so check the manual before using one.

Never mix cleaning products, and never use a descaler that the maker does not approve. After the flush, run clean water through the unit until the smell of vinegar is gone, then check for leaks at the service valves before you restore power and gas.

Warning signs

Signs scale is building up

Water takes longer to get hot

Scale slows heat transfer.

Temperature swings

Flow through the exchanger is uneven.

Error codes

Sensors read overheating or low flow.

Noise from the unit

Scale and boiling can cause rumbling.

If you see these signs, flush the unit and look at the water. Our tankless water heater page covers repairs and installation as well.

Checklist

What you need for a tankless flush

  • A small submersible pump and two hoses with the right fittings.
  • A five gallon bucket and enough white vinegar to cover the pump intake.
  • Isolation valves on the hot and cold lines, or a plan to add them.
  • The manual for your unit, for any model-specific steps.
  • A towel and a flashlight for the service valves.

If any item on this list is missing, a plumber can supply it and handle the flush in about an hour. For a unit that has never been flushed, a plumber can also check how heavy the scale is and whether the unit has been running hot.

Mistakes

Mistakes to avoid when flushing a tankless unit

Skipping the manual is the first. Some heat exchangers do not tolerate certain descalers, and a wrong product can cause damage that voids a warranty.

Leaving the service valves in the wrong position after the flush is the other. Check that the valves are fully open, the gas and power are back on and the unit fires normally before you leave it.

Local notes

Water in Euless and what to ask

Euless notes on water supply

Euless buys its main water supply from the Trinity River Authority, which draws from Cedar Creek and Richland Chambers reservoirs, and the city also operates wells in the Trinity Aquifer, according to its public works page. The mix can change, and so can the mineral content at your tap.

The city posts annual water quality reports. Read the latest, test your own tap and use the results to set a flush schedule, and ask your plumber about a softener if the numbers are high.
