What we install

Fixtures, appliances and new lines

Most installation calls fall into a few groups. New fixtures include sinks, toilets, tubs, showers and faucets, and appliance hookups cover dishwashers, washing machines, ice makers and water heaters.

Larger jobs add new supply lines, drain branches and vents for remodels, additions and finished garages. A plumber works from plans or from a walk-through so the pipes meet the fixtures in the right places.

Planning

Planning a plumbing project before the walls open

The best time to fix plumbing is before the drywall goes up. A remodel is a chance to replace old shutoff valves, upgrade a worn supply line and move a fixture that was badly placed.

Bring the plumber in at the design stage. Where a drain can run depends on the slope, and where a vent can go depends on the framing, so a layout that looks good on paper may need adjustments.

Code

Permits, inspections and doing it to code

Many plumbing installations require a permit and an inspection. Rules depend on the type of work and the address, so ask the City of Euless building department what applies, and tell the plumber early so the schedule allows for it.

The Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners licenses plumbers in the state, and you can verify any plumber's license on the board's public search before hiring. A permit and a licensed plumber protect you if the work is ever questioned in a sale or an insurance claim.

Before we arrive

How to prepare for an installation visit

Clear a path to the work area and empty the cabinet under the sink or the closet around the heater. Know where your main water shutoff is, and test it ahead of time so it turns without a fight.

Have the fixture, its box and the installation sheet on hand if you bought it yourself, and tell the plumber about any past leaks in the same area. A few minutes of preparation often saves a return trip for a missing part.

Ask questions before the work starts, including how long water will be off and what the plumber will do if a hidden problem turns up behind a wall. A clear answer up front prevents surprises on the bill.

New construction

New builds, additions and finished garages

A new addition needs water, drain and vent lines tied into the existing system. A finished garage with a bathroom or laundry needs a drain at the right slope and a vent that reaches the roof or ties into another line.
