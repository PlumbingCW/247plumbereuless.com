Sewer Backup in Euless, TX: What to Do First

A sewer backup is stressful, smelly and unsanitary. The first few minutes matter, because wastewater keeps coming as long as water runs in the house.

This guide lists what to do first, who to call and how to tell whether the blockage is on your side or the city's.

Act now

The first ten minutes

Stop all water use. Do not flush, run the washer, take a shower or use the dishwasher, because every gallon adds to the flood.

Keep children and pets out of the area, and avoid touching wastewater with bare hands. Open windows if the smell is strong, and move rugs, electronics and boxes away from the wet floor.

Do not do these

Do not run a plunger hard on a backed-up tub, because it can push wastewater back through other drains. Do not pour chemical drain openers into a fully backed-up line, since the chemicals can sit in the pipe and splash on the plumber.

Diagnose

Which fixtures are backing up

The lowest fixture in the house, often a tub, shower or floor drain, backs up first. If several fixtures back up together, the main line is blocked.

What you seeWhat it suggests
One sink or tub slowA local clog in that branch
Toilet bubbles when another fixture drainsPartial blockage in the main
Floor drain or tub fills when the washer runsMain line blocked
Smell in the yard or a wet spotBroken line or overflow at a cleanout

Tell the dispatcher which fixtures are backing up. If every drain is affected, our sewer line cleaning team starts with the main.

City or private

Is the blockage yours or the city's

Your private lateral runs from the house to the point where it meets the city's main. A blockage in the lateral is yours to fix, and a blockage in the city's main is the city's responsibility.

A plumber can run a camera or a cable to see which side the problem is on. If the line is clear up to the city's connection, the city should be called with that information.

If the camera shows a cracked or collapsed pipe on your side, a sewer line repair may be needed after the clog is cleared.

Roots and age

Why older lines back up more

Older lines tend to have more joints, and joints are where roots enter. Clay and fiber pipes can also shift or deform over decades, and cast iron can corrode from the inside.

A backup in an older house is a reason to look at the line, not only to clear it. Clearing a root ball without finding where it entered means the roots return, often within a year.

Cleanup

Cleaning up and protecting your health

Wastewater carries bacteria and viruses, so wear gloves and boots, and ventilate the room. Hard surfaces can be washed with a disinfectant, but carpet and padding soaked by sewage usually need to be removed.

For large spills or a flooded basement-level space, a cleanup company can extract water and dry the area. Do not delay, since mold can start within a day or two.

Prevention

Reducing the chance of another backup

Most backups have a cause you can reduce. Keep grease out of kitchen drains, flush only toilet paper, and watch for slow drains that return after clearing, because they often signal buildup or roots in the line.

If the line has backed up more than once, ask a plumber for a camera inspection. The video shows whether roots, a belly or a cracked joint is at work, and a repair or a periodic hydro jetting visit can break the cycle.

A backwater valve is a device some homes install to block wastewater from flowing back up from the main. Whether one suits your house depends on the plumbing layout and local rules, so ask a plumber and the city building department before deciding.

Checklist

What to have ready when you call

  • Your address and the location of the cleanout, if you know it.
  • Which fixtures are backing up and since when.
  • Whether the backup began after heavy rain, a flush or a laundry load.
  • Whether any neighbors are having the same problem.
  • Photos of the affected area for your records.

The question about neighbors is worth asking. If a neighbor is also backing up, the city's main is more likely the cause, and a quick call to the city can save you the cost of a visit that will not fix the problem.

Mistakes

Mistakes to avoid during a sewer backup

Running water to see if the drain clears makes the flood bigger. Wait until a plumber has cleared the line before you use any fixtures.

Another is skipping photos in the rush to clean up. Take pictures first, because an insurance claim is much easier with a clear record of the damage.

Documentation

Photos, records and insurance

Photograph the damage before you clean, note the time you first noticed the backup and keep the plumber's invoice and camera video. Sewer backup coverage often requires an endorsement on a homeowners policy, so call your insurer and ask.

Keep a list of damaged items with approximate ages and values. It makes a claim faster and more accurate.

Euless notes on who to call for a city main backup

The City of Euless public works utility division takes wastewater backup reports on weekdays from 7 a.m. to 5 p.m. and provides 24-hour service for after-hours and weekend emergencies, according to its public works FAQ. If a plumber's camera shows your line is clear to the connection, report the backup to the division with that information.

Neighboring Hurst runs a 24-hour public works dispatch for water and wastewater problems, and the same private-versus-city question applies there, as described on our Hurst page.
