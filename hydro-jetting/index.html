What it is

What hydro jetting does that a cable cannot

A jetter pumps water through a hose with a nozzle that fires jets backward, which both pulls the hose forward and sprays the pipe wall. A drain cable cuts a hole through the clog, but the nozzle washes the whole circumference of the pipe.

That matters for kitchen lines and older cast iron, where grease and scale narrow the pipe to a fraction of its real size. Restoring the full diameter means the line drains faster and takes much longer to clog again.

Urgent

Sewer backup: when jetting is the emergency fix

A main line that backs up into a tub or floor drain needs fast action, and jetting is often the right tool when a cable cannot move the blockage. Hydro jetting is a common response to grease-packed lines, heavy scale and root hairs, as long as the pipe is structurally sound.

If wastewater is already on the floor, stop using water and keep people and pets away from it. Tell the dispatcher what is backing up and where the cleanout is, so the plumber can bring the right equipment on the first trip.

Before the plumber arrives

Locate the cleanout cap, usually a round plug near the house wall or along the front of the lot, and clear a path to it. Move vehicles off the area if you can, and keep the washer and dishwasher off so nothing new enters the line.

Process

How a jetting visit works

1

Camera first

A camera shows what the line holds and whether it is sound enough for pressure.

2

Jet to size

The technician chooses a nozzle and pressure that match the pipe material.

3

Wash and flush

The nozzle scours the wall while debris is flushed to the main.

4

Verify

A second camera pass confirms the pipe is open and shows any damage.

Jetting uses pressure suited to the pipe, because the water is aimed along the wall and not at one spot. A line that shows a crack, a collapsed section or a belly on camera needs repair, not more pressure.

Roots

Roots, scale and grease: what jetting handles

Tree roots enter through joints and cracks, and fine root hairs can be flushed out by a jet. Thick, woody roots need a cable with a cutter blade first, and then jetting removes what the blade leaves behind.

Mature trees along older streets near Little Bear Creek and the Euless neighborhoods around Heritage Park are common root sources, so a camera pass tells you whether a root path or a failed joint caused the stoppage. If the camera finds a crack, a sewer line repair stops the roots from returning.

When not to jet

Jetting is not right for every pipe

Water pressure can make a cracked or offset line worse, and a collapsed pipe cannot be cleared by any tool. A camera inspection before jetting prevents a small repair from becoming a flooded crawl space or yard.

For a simple trap or sink clog, a drain cleaning cable is quicker and cheaper. For a line that has already failed, the plan shifts to repair. For a line that is open but narrowed by grease, jetting restores the pipe without digging.
