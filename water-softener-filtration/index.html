Start with a test

Test your tap before buying a system

Mineral hardness is the reason for most scale, and it varies by water supply. Euless buys its main supply from the Trinity River Authority, which draws from Cedar Creek and Richland Chambers reservoirs, and the city also operates wells in the Trinity Aquifer, according to its public works page.

The city's annual water quality report describes what is in the water as it leaves the city's system, and a home test shows what arrives at your tap. Use both to decide whether a softener, a filter or nothing at all fits your home.

Systems

Softener, filter or both

A salt-based softener removes the minerals that cause scale and can protect water heaters and fixtures. A salt-free conditioner changes how minerals behave but does not remove them, so results differ.

A whole-house carbon filter treats water before it reaches every tap, and a point-of-use filter at the kitchen sink is a lower-cost option for drinking water. Some homes use a filter and a softener together.

Repair

Softener and conditioner repairs

A softener that stops softening may have a salt bridge, an empty brine tank, a clogged injector or a control valve fault. A salt bridge is a hard crust in the salt that stops the brine from forming, and breaking it often restores the unit.

If a unit is old and failing repeatedly, replacement may be cheaper than another repair. We service common brands and explain what is worth fixing.

Living with it

Maintaining a softener or filter

A salt-based softener needs salt added to the brine tank, and the level should be checked every month or two. Use the salt type the manufacturer recommends, because some products leave residue that clogs the injector.

Whole-house filters need new cartridges on a schedule, and a filter that has gone too long between changes can restrict flow and hold back contaminants rather than catching them. Write the change dates on the housing so you do not have to remember.

Water treatment equipment drains to the household sewer, so the discharge has to run to a proper drain with an air gap. Ask the plumber to show you the bypass valve, in case you need to isolate the unit during a repair.
