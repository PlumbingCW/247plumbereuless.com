Signs you have a hidden leak

Most leaks announce themselves by symptom. Callers describe a water bill that doubled, a meter that keeps moving, a damp patch on a ceiling, a warm spot on a floor, a sound of running water at night or water pressure that has dropped.

Meter keeps moving

With every fixture off, a moving meter or a spinning low-flow dial means water is escaping.

Bill jumps with no change

A sudden rise with no new use points to a leak.

Stain or bubble on a ceiling or wall

Water has traveled from a pipe above or behind the surface.

Warm or damp floor spot

A hot-water line leaking under the slab can warm the floor above it.

Low pressure at every tap

A leak on the main or service line can sap pressure through the whole house.

Urgent

Hidden leak emergency: when to shut off the main

A leak is an emergency when water is coming through a ceiling, when the floor is wet and spreading or when you hear running water you cannot trace. Turn the main water valve clockwise to shut off the house supply, then call.

Shutting the main stops the flow while the plumber is on the way and buys time to protect belongings. Our dispatcher can send a licensed plumber the same day when one is available, including nights and weekends.

Do this first

Close the main valve, open a faucet at the lowest point to drain remaining water and move valuables away from wet areas. Take dated photos of the damage for your insurance record, and switch off power to a wet room only if you can reach the breaker without standing in water.

Meter test

The water meter test for a high bill

You can do a simple check before you call. Turn off every fixture, appliance and irrigation zone, then read the meter or watch the low-flow dial for ten to fifteen minutes. If the dial moves with everything off, water is escaping somewhere between the meter and the house or inside it.

A leak on the yard side of the meter may belong to the city, while a leak between the meter and the house is private. Hurst's public works page, for example, states that the city owns the line to the meter and the customer owns the line from the meter to the home, and Euless homeowners should ask their own city about the same boundary.

Method

How leaks are found without tearing things open

A plumber starts with a pressure test to confirm that the leak is real and narrow down which line holds it. Acoustic listening gear picks up the hiss of escaping water through pipe and slab, and a thermal camera shows the temperature pattern of a hot-water leak under a floor.

Tracer gas and line-by-line isolation come next if the sound alone does not give a position. The goal is a mark on the floor or in the yard where the repair is made, so the opening is small.

Where leaks hide

Ceiling, wall, under-house and buried lines

A ceiling or wall leak often comes from a supply line, a drain or an upstairs fixture, and the stain is below the source. In a home on a pier-and-beam foundation, a leak in the crawl space can soak the subfloor, and in a slab home the lines run beneath the foundation, which is covered on our slab leak page.

Buried lines between the meter and the house, and sprinkler or yard lines, can leak for months before the ground shows it. A water service line repair fixes a private line that has failed. Irrigation leaks and pool or spa lines are checked as part of the visit when you ask.

After the find

Repair, insurance and documentation

Once the leak is located, a repair can often be done in the same visit. If the line is old galvanized or has failed repeatedly, a repiping quote shows the long-term option.

Ask for a written note of where the leak was and what was repaired, along with photos. Coverage rules differ by policy, and burst or sudden damage is treated differently from slow seepage, so call your insurer with the facts in hand.
