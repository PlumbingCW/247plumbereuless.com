Bathtub Faucet Repair: A Euless, TX Homeowner Guide

A bathtub faucet that drips after you shut it off wastes water and stains the tub. A faucet that will not shut off at all is more urgent, because it can run all night.

This guide explains the common causes, the repairs a homeowner can try and the situations where a plumber is the better call.

Diagnosis

What your tub faucet is telling you

Most tub faucet problems start inside the valve behind the wall. A drip from the spout, a handle that is hard to turn or a shower that goes hot and cold all trace back to a worn part in that valve.

SymptomLikely causeCommon fix
Drips from the spout after shutoffWorn cartridge, washer or seatReplace the cartridge or washer
Handle stiff or looseCorroded stem or loose set screwClean, tighten or replace the stem
Hot and cold swingsWorn pressure-balancing cartridgeReplace the cartridge
Water at the wallLeaking valve body or connectionOpen the wall and repair
Types

Know which kind of valve you have

Tub faucets use compression, cartridge, ball or ceramic disc designs. A compression faucet has separate hot and cold handles that screw down on a washer, and a single-handle faucet usually uses a cartridge.

The brand and model decide which parts you need. Remove the handle and look at the cartridge, then match it by photo or by the number stamped on it before you buy a replacement.

Safety

Scalding and temperature swings

A shower that goes suddenly hot is more than a nuisance, especially for children and older adults. Set your water heater to about 120 degrees Fahrenheit to reduce scalding risk, and replace a pressure-balancing cartridge that no longer holds a steady temperature.

If the temperature changes when someone flushes a toilet or runs the washing machine, the cartridge or the supply pressure may be at fault. A plumber can test both and tell you which.

DIY repair

Steps to replace a cartridge

1

Shut off the water

Use the shutoff valve or the main, then open the faucet to drain.

2

Remove the handle

Take off the cap and the screw, then pull the handle off.

3

Pull the cartridge

Remove the retaining clip or nut and pull the cartridge straight out.

4

Install a matching one

Grease the O-rings, seat the new cartridge and reassemble.

Protect the tub with a towel, because a dropped tool can chip the finish. If the cartridge will not move, stop before you force it, because a seized valve body can crack.

Hard parts

When the repair is more than a cartridge

A valve body that is corroded, a missing shutoff or a leak inside the wall means the job is bigger. Accessing the valve may require opening the wall from behind or removing tile.

A plumber will tell you whether an access panel is available. If a leak is inside the wall, early action prevents rot and mold. Our faucet and fixture repair page explains what is typical, and a hidden leak may call for leak detection.

Prevention

Keeping a tub faucet working longer

Turn handles gently and avoid cranking them past the point where the water stops. Forcing a handle compresses the washer or scores the seat inside the valve, and that is how a small drip starts.

Wipe mineral deposits off the spout and showerhead every few months with a vinegar-soaked cloth, and clear the aerator or diverter if flow is uneven. Tell a plumber about any handle that has become stiff, because stiffness is often the first sign of a corroding stem.

If your tub and shower share a valve, a worn cartridge affects both. Replacing the cartridge on a schedule, rather than waiting for a failure, can stop temperature swings before someone gets scalded.

Checklist

Before you take the handle off

  • Shut off the water at the fixture stops or the main valve.
  • Put a towel in the tub to catch screws and protect the finish.
  • Photograph each step so you can reassemble in the right order.
  • Bring the old cartridge or a clear photo of it to the store.
  • Have a spare set of O-rings and plumber's grease ready.

Taking a few photos as you go is the habit that separates a clean repair from a pile of parts. Faucet makers use different retaining clips, set screws and stem designs, and an hour of guessing is easy to avoid when you can look back at the previous step.

Mistakes

Mistakes to avoid when repairing a tub faucet

Forcing a stuck handle or cartridge can crack the valve body, which turns a cheap repair into a wall repair. If a part will not move with steady pressure, stop and ask a plumber.

Buying a part without matching it is the other one. Cartridges look alike but differ in length, splines and seals, so compare the old part to the new one before you install it.

Emergency

Faucet that will not shut off

If water will not stop, shut the main water valve and call. A tub faucet that keeps running can overflow the tub and flood the floor below.

Do not wait to see whether it stops. A bathtub fed by a failed valve can pour out gallons an hour, and the damage grows with each hour.
