Why

When a sewer inspection makes sense

Three situations account for most inspections. A buyer wants to know what the line looks like before closing, an owner has had a clog return, or a plumber has quoted a repair and the owner wants to confirm the diagnosis.

Inspections are planned rather than urgent, but the findings can save thousands in a bad surprise. If water is already coming up into the house, start with sewer line repair or sewer line cleaning and add the camera to the visit.

Process

How the camera inspection works

1

Find access

A cleanout or a pulled toilet gives a way into the line.

2

Push the camera

A cable carries the camera along the pipe while it records.

3

Locate problems

A locator marks the depth and position of anything found.

4

Review it

You watch the video and get a plain explanation of what it shows.

A good inspection records the whole run to the connection with the city main and notes the pipe material and any offsets. Ask for the video file, because it is useful for a repair quote and for negotiations in a home sale.

Reading the video

What the camera can show

A clean pipe looks smooth and continuous. Roots appear as white or tan hairs at joints, grease as a pale coating along the wall and cracks as dark lines that may widen toward a missing piece.

What you seeWhat it meansLikely next step
Fine roots at a jointA small gap is letting in moistureCut roots and consider lining
Standing water in a dipA belly where waste collectsReplace the sagging section
Offset jointPipe sections no longer alignSpot repair or lining

Buying a home

Inspecting the sewer line when you buy

A general home inspection usually does not include a sewer camera. Older neighborhoods in North Texas can have lines that have shifted with clay soil, and a repair can run into the thousands, so asking for the video before closing is a reasonable step.

The camera does not tell you who is responsible for a defect. Your agent and the sales contract decide that, so share the video with them promptly.

Records

Keeping the video and notes

Save the inspection video with the date and your address, and keep any notes the plumber writes about depth, material and defects. If a repair is done later, a second video shows the result and gives you a before and after record.

When you sell the house, a recent clear inspection is a useful document for the buyer, while a recorded defect shows what was disclosed and when.

After

What happens after the inspection

If the pipe is clear, you have a record of its condition and a baseline for later. If it shows problems, the plumber explains the options and prices before any work starts, and you decide.

Lines with heavy buildup can be cleaned right away with hydro jetting. Lines with structural damage need repair, which may include lining or replacement of the damaged section.
