Signs

Signs of a slab leak

Slab leaks are hidden by design, so the evidence is indirect. Callers describe a warm patch on a tile or a hardwood floor, a water bill that jumped, a sound of running water when everything is off, a damp carpet or cracks in the grout and baseboards.

Warm spot on the floor

A leaking hot-water line heats the slab above it.

Running meter, all taps off

Water is leaving the system somewhere.

Damp flooring or musty smell

Water is rising through the slab or the joints.

Low pressure

A large leak can lower pressure throughout the house.

Urgent

What to do when water comes through the floor

If water is visible on the floor and rising, shut off the main water valve at the house or at the street side of the house, where your supply enters. The meter box valve at the curb belongs to the utility, so use the home shutoff first.

Turn off the water heater to protect it from running dry, lift valuables off the floor and call. Our dispatcher can send a plumber the same day when one is available, including nights and weekends.

Protect the home while you wait

Close the main valve, switch off the water heater, move rugs and furniture off the wet floor and photograph the damage. Run a fan if you have one to start the drying.

Why here

Why North Texas slabs leak

Most North Texas homes are built on slab foundations, and the soil beneath them is expansive clay. Clay swells when wet and shrinks when dry, and the slab moves with it, which can stress pipes that run through or beneath it.

Pipe material and workmanship matter too. Copper under a slab can corrode, and a line rubbing against concrete or a gravel bed can wear through over years. Dry spells followed by heavy rain are a classic time for foundation movement, so a sudden new leak after the weather swings is not unusual.

Finding it

How we find a slab leak

A plumber begins by isolating the system to confirm that water is leaking, then uses acoustic listening, thermal imaging and pressure tests to narrow the location. The goal is a mark on the floor within a foot or two of the leak.

Meter and pressure testConfirms a leak and the line it is on

Repair

Repair options: break the slab, reroute or line

A spot repair opens the slab over the leak, replaces the damaged section and patches the concrete. A reroute abandons the failed line and runs a new one through the attic or walls, which avoids concrete but opens drywall.

If the house has repeated leaks or aging copper, a repipe can address all the lines. A line that is leaking only at one spot on a sound system is usually best repaired in place.

Insurance

Insurance, flooring and documentation

Slab leak coverage varies by policy. Some policies pay for the resulting water damage and for access to the pipe but not for the pipe repair itself, so ask your insurer before work begins.

Keep the plumber's written report, the photos and any moisture readings. If flooring needs replacement, the report supports the claim.
