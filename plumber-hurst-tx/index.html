Hurst at a glance

Emergency plumbing for a Mid-Cities neighbor

Hurst is a Mid-Cities city in Tarrant County that incorporated on September 25, 1952. Bell Helicopter opened a plant in Hurst in 1951, and the town's population passed 20,000 by the mid-1960s, according to the city's library history.

A city that grew that fast has a lot of housing that is now decades old, and older houses bring older plumbing. Drain lines, water heaters and supply pipes that have served for decades are worth a plumber's eye.

Before we arrive

If you have a burst pipe or an active leak in Hurst

A few steps in the first minutes limit the damage while a plumber is on the way. Do them in order, and call the 24/7 line as soon as the water is under control.

Shut off the main valve

Turn the main water valve clockwise until it stops. It is usually near the meter, in a garage or on an exterior wall.

Kill power to wet areas

Switch off breakers for any room with standing water, but only if you can reach the panel without stepping in water.

Clear the area

Lift rugs, electronics and boxes off the floor, and set out buckets under active drips.

Water and utilities

Who owns the water line in Hurst

The City of Hurst says it owns the water line from the main to the meter and the customer owns the line from the meter to the house. A leak on the yard side of the meter is a private repair, and Hurst asks homeowners planning to replace the private portion to notify the city.

Hurst's water division maintains about 205 miles of water lines and 12,100 meters, and the city runs a 24-hour public works dispatch for water and wastewater problems. If you are unsure whether a leak is yours or the city's, a plumber can test the private side with a pressure test, and the city can be called for the rest.

Hurst has also published a service line inventory, so you can look up your street and number to see what the city has recorded for your service line. That is a useful check if your house is older and you are wondering what material your line is made of.
